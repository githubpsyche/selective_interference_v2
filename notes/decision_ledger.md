**Locked Or Current Working Decisions**

- Title: “A retrieved-context account of selective interference in intrusive and voluntary remembering.”
- Main thesis: selective interference can reflect retrieval cues and control, not only differently vulnerable memory representations.
- Avoid making “single system” the headline claim.
- Do not deny that the intervention changes learned representations; in this model, task items become associated with film-related context.
- The key contrast is not “representations never matter” but “the behavioral dissociation is not self-interpreting evidence about separately vulnerable memory formats.”
- Recognition is central to motivation and early framing, but appears last in the simulation sequence as a test-format boundary condition.
- No standalone VIT/test-phase cue simulation section in this draft.
- No Simulation 5/task-specificity section; task specificity is Discussion/future work.
- Include all selected figures in the working draft for collaborator review.
- Simulation 1 establishes reminder-linked task competition during context-guided recall.
- Simulation 2 shows that deliberate recall is less affected only under particular control settings, especially a maintained film-retrieval goal.
- Several control mechanisms may operate during deliberate recall, but only film-goal/source-like support is protective against reminder-linked task competition in the current simulations.
- Recognition appears spared because the item is presented and evaluated against context rather than selected among film and task candidates.
- VIT/VRT nulls should be discussed as possible cases where film cues, shared lab context, or monitoring instructions make intrusion-like and voluntary tests rely on more similar film-directed retrieval states.

**Global Failure Modes**

- The draft says or implies that CMR disproves DRA.
- The draft treats voluntary memory as one computational category.
- The draft treats recognition as context-free or globally immune.
- Recognition appears only as an afterthought.
- The model reads as “we simulated Tetris” rather than a retrieved-context account of retrieval conditions.
- The source/goal mechanism looks like an arbitrary film boost.
- Test-phase film cues and film-retrieval goals are privileged inconsistently.
- Simulation results are presented as quantitative fits to trauma-film data.
- VIT/VRT or task-specificity claims become unsimulated results rather than discussion boundary conditions.
