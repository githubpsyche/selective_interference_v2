While working on my newest text, I've noticed that our new dependence on the maintained retrieval goal mechanism to simulate the selective interference effect produces a model with some marked similarities to the dual representation account itself. Like the DRA, our framework similarly proposes that:

1) **Even under matched conditions, deliberate and unguided retrieval differentially rely on two ways to retrieve stored memories of trauma film clips.** In our model, those are "temporal" context-to-item associations and "source" context-to-item associations, and deliberate recall but not unguided recall specially exploits the latter. 
2) **The reminder-linked visuospatial task intervention messes with one of these retrieval pathways but not the other.** In our model, the reminder-linked visuospatial task intervention makes film-associated temporal context states also a good cue for visuospatial task material, implying competitive interference whenever retrieval is largely context-guided. 
3) **Deliberate recall is comparatively protected from reminder-linked task interference compared to unguided recall because it can use the undisturbed retrieval pathway**.

Of course, when you get in the details, there's still plenty that's distinct between our proposal and the DRA. And our model can still be interpreted as a single-system account of the selective interference effect. Indeed, if the manuscript were entirely focused on recognition as a voluntary memory test (as most of the selective interference literature is), then the goal-maintenance mechanism would not even be necessary. And minimum, our account seems distinctly more focused on retrieval conditions and control mechanisms to address the effect than on memory representations.

Still, these analogies have made me uncomfortable with our previous strategy to focally present the manuscript as providing a "single-system account" of the dissociation. We have a retrieved-context account. Maybe it's cleaner to just lean into that. There are other more dramatic options that come to mind, but this more narrow tact is what I've taken in my recent revisions. But perhaps I'm overthinking it!
