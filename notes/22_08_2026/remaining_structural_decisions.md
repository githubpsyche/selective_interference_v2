## 2. What does the control-mechanism simulation claim?

### Structural issue

The original Results could be read either as a contest in which the maintained category cue happens to win at selected parameters, or as a decomposition in which all three forms of control are equally capable of explaining selective interference. Rik’s feedback requires a clearer answer: which operations produce the selective interaction, which merely raise recall, and how could their consequences be distinguished?

### Direct feedback and Jordan’s criterion

- **Rik comment 316, 25 July 12:05**, asks:

  > Does the insufficiency of start-of-film and monitoring (in the absence of category cue) hold across all parameter settings for start-reinstatement and monitoring? Presumably not—so their insufficiency is just in the context of default parameter settings?

- **Rik comments 321, 322, 324, and 333** ask for matched positional and output-order diagnostics and explain that their purpose is to distinguish the three mechanisms empirically.
- **Rik comment 350** says the mechanisms could co-occur and asks the paper to explain how positional data could test them.
- **Jordan reply comment 42, 27 July 10:44**, states the criterion:

  > The selective interference effect provides a demanding test: successful control must reduce the effect of interference, not merely raise recall overall.

These comments are transcribed with their anchors and timestamps in the analysis [feedback trace](/Users/jordangunn/workspace/selective_interference_v2/work/simulation2_control_parameter_sensitivity/FEEDBACK_TRACE.md).

### Analysis support

The completed [sensitivity analysis](/Users/jordangunn/workspace/selective_interference_v2/work/simulation2_control_parameter_sensitivity/results/RESULTS.md) evaluates the full declared 11 × 11 effective start-reinstatement × monitoring grid under fixed fitted base parameters.

- With the category cue absent, **0/121** regular-grid settings produced positive reminder-specific retrieval × reminder × task protection.
- The manuscript’s current no-cue setting produced P = −0.455, 95% Monte Carlo interval [−0.521, −0.388].
- Some extreme settings improved weaker reminder-only or endpoint contrasts, but those effects were not the selective-interference interaction and accompanied large generic recall gains.
- The maintained category cue alone produced positive three-way protection, P = .617.
- All three controls together produced P = .792.

### Side-thread conclusion and main-task resolution

Jordan supplied the completed side-thread analysis to the main task on **10 August at 17:46**. The side-thread conclusion was:

> Within the fixed fitted model regime, the maintained category cue is necessary for the selective-interference interaction. Start reinstatement and monitoring remain potentially co-occurring operations with distinct input-position and output-transition signatures. Present this as a decomposition, not a horse race—but do not imply that all three mechanisms are equally capable of explaining the selective interaction.

This wording is a side-thread synthesis rather than a collaborator comment or verbatim Jordan statement. Its substantive claim is independently constrained by the reproducible results above. The main-task synthesis at 17:52 accepted it as an **asymmetric decomposition** after Jordan asked whether the supplied handoffs cleared the structural decisions. The durable record is the [reorientation transcript](/Users/jordangunn/.codex/sessions/2026/08/10/rollout-2026-08-10T13-16-46-019feb9a-9df0-7231-bf10-a14d51a6c0bd.jsonl).

### Commitment for the next version

- Present the maintained category cue as necessary for the selective-interference interaction **within the evaluated fixed fitted regime**.
- Present start reinstatement and monitoring as potentially co-occurring controls that can augment recall and generate distinct signatures, not as equally successful explanations of the selective interaction.
- Keep selective protection separate from generic recall improvement and from weaker reminder-only or endpoint contrasts.
- Use matched input-position and output-transition diagnostics to show how the implemented mechanisms could be distinguished.
- Do not generalize necessity beyond the evaluated grid to all possible eCMR parameterizations.

### Implementation readiness

This is fully resolved at the claim level. The feedback trace, estimand, numerical results, verification, claim boundary, and proposed main-versus-supplement division are already specified. Remaining choices are editorial, such as how many numerical details belong in the main text.

**Provenance strength:** very strong; direct feedback, Jordan’s criterion, reproducible analysis, and the recorded resolution agree.

- In the side-thread handoff supplied on **10 August at 17:46**, Jordan clarified the paper’s positive proposal:

  > I guess the more positive proposal in my paper is that the neutral/emotional split doesn’t matter; you should get these effects with neutral items as long as the post-reminder intervention and study period are sufficiently distinct.

  The side-thread assistant’s accompanying formulation was:

  > Emotion is neither necessary nor sufficient. Shared retrieval context creates competition; target-diagnostic information allows deliberate recall to escape it.

  The first quotation is Jordan’s own language because it was explicitly labeled “ME” in the handoff. The second is assistant synthesis. The durable record is the [reorientation transcript](/Users/jordangunn/.codex/sessions/2026/08/10/rollout-2026-08-10T13-16-46-019feb9a-9df0-7231-bf10-a14d51a6c0bd.jsonl). The original side-task transcript was not independently identified.

### Commitment for the next version

- Do **not** add a standalone emotional-versus-neutral simulation as a new main Results section.
- State the positive generality claim: emotionality is not required for selective interference.
- Explain the operative configuration as **contextually entangled but feature-distinct**: reminder-linked intervention material becomes a competitor, while target-diagnostic information allows deliberate recall to favor study material.
- Predict selective interference with neutral study and intervention material when they are contextually connected but remain distinguishable by the target-directed cue.
- Predict reduced deliberate protection when neutral intervention material also matches the target cue.
- Allow emotion to change baseline accessibility or effect magnitude without treating it as necessary or sufficient for the dissociation.
- Treat a neutral simulation, if run, as supplementary robustness. A stronger test would vary target–competitor discriminability within neutral material.

### Implementation readiness

The main manuscript decision is resolved: no new principal emotion simulation, and the Discussion should state the neutral-material generality prediction. Whether to run and formally report an optional supplementary robustness check remains open.

**Provenance strength:** strong for excluding a main simulation and for the generality claim; the optional analysis is undecided.
