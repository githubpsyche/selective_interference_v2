## 2. The paper’s central thesis

**Anchor:** New Jordan root comment on the Introduction sentence beginning “To address this gap, we model unguided and deliberate recall…” on page 5.

**Comment 1 — How this came up**

> Deborah repeatedly asked me to make the contribution larger and clearer: to “make a bigger point” of the general memory issue, introduce the “main innovation—simulation of deliberate recall,” and explain that the question is larger than this particular intervention task. The August meeting notes summarize the intended framing as “what deliberate means” and “a deconstruction of the idea of deliberate recall.” Rik’s Discussion comments likewise ask the paper to return to the importance of the problem, the existing dual-representation explanation, the retrieved-context alternative, and the possibility that several retrieval-control operations coexist. The issue is therefore not simply whether the model can reproduce a trauma-film result. It is what this result reveals about how the same memory system produces different outcomes under different retrieval goals and test formats.

**Comment 2 — What I have decided**

> The paper’s theoretical spine will be the question of what makes remembering deliberate. The trauma-film selective-interference effect will be presented as the consequential and demanding test case: the model must explain how the same learned film–task associations can leave material less likely to enter retrieval without a target-directed goal while allowing relatively successful goal-directed remembering. I am choosing this framing because it states the general contribution without abandoning the empirical problem that motivates it. It also makes clear that preserved voluntary performance need not imply that a separate memory representation escaped interference; it may instead result from the way retrieval is cued, initiated, and controlled.

**Comment 3 — What changes in the manuscript**

> The Abstract will name the general question before introducing the trauma-film application. The Introduction’s gap paragraph will state explicitly that retrieved-context models have not previously been used to explain how one set of learned associations can yield different interference effects across involuntary retrieval, deliberate recall, and deliberate recognition. The end of the Introduction will describe selective interference as the test of that account, rather than as the full contribution. The General Discussion will open by returning to this question before summarizing individual simulations, and the final paragraph will state what the account adds to general theories of episodic search. A suitable recurring formulation is: “Selective interference provides a demanding test of how retrieval goals and test format make remembering deliberate within a single episodic-memory system.”

## 4. Replacing “unguided”

**Anchor:** Reply to Emily’s existing “involuntary?” comment at the first model comparison in the Abstract, page 2.

**Reply 1 — How this came up**

> My earlier terminology policy used “unguided recall” for the simplified model condition while reserving “intrusions” for empirical reports. Emily subsequently asked for “involuntary” here and later reminded me to change the term in the graphs. The August meeting likewise records “involuntary vs deliberate,” notes that “guided” can itself mean experimenter-guided, and asks for consistent involuntary/voluntary framing. The remaining issue is not whether to keep “unguided”—I will not—but how to adopt “involuntary” without implying that the model reproduces diary or vigilance-based intrusion reports.

**Reply 2 — What I have decided**

> At its first definition, I will call the condition the **involuntary-retrieval analogue**. Thereafter the prose will use **the involuntary-retrieval condition**, and figures will use the shorter display label **Involuntary retrieval**. The intentional recall test will remain **deliberate recall**, and the item-presenting test will remain **deliberate recognition**. “Voluntary memory” will refer to the empirical literature’s broader category of intentional tests, while “goal-directed remembering” will name the general theoretical construct. “Intrusions” will be reserved for empirical reports or genuinely off-target outputs. This follows Emily’s and the meeting’s preferred framing while preserving the boundary between the empirical phenomenon and its simplified model analogue.

**Reply 3 — What changes in the manuscript**

> The first definition will say: “The involuntary-retrieval condition omits a maintained target goal and serves as a simplified analogue of film material coming to mind without deliberate retrieval; it is not a simulation of diary or vigilance-based intrusion reports.” Every occurrence of “unguided recall” or “unguided retrieval” in the Abstract, main text, headings, captions, legends, and axis labels will then be reviewed and replaced with the appropriate form—not changed through a blind global substitution. Parenthetical equations such as “unguided film recall (intrusions)” will be removed. Captions will use “Involuntary retrieval” versus “Deliberate recall,” and the Discussion will retain the limitation that empirical involuntary-memory procedures contain cues and monitoring demands absent from the model condition.

## 5. What the control analysis establishes

**Anchor:** Reply to Rik’s existing parameter-setting comment on the Figure 8 decomposition, page 47.

**Reply 1 — How this came up**

> Rik asks whether start-of-film reinstatement and monitoring remain insufficient when their settings are varied, rather than only at the manuscript’s chosen defaults. His related comments ask for positional and output-order analyses that would show how the three proposed operations could be distinguished empirically even if they co-occur. My earlier reply established the relevant criterion: a successful explanation must reduce the reminder-specific selective-interference interaction, not merely increase recall generally. The issue is therefore which operation explains the relative protection of deliberate recall in the tested model, and which operations instead produce other improvements or diagnostic signatures.

**Reply 2 — What the follow-up analysis showed**

> A reproducible follow-up crossed the complete declared 11 × 11 grid of effective start reinstatement and monitoring strength, with the maintained category cue absent, using 5,000 trials per cell and ten paired Monte Carlo batches. None of the 121 settings produced positive reminder-specific selective protection. The manuscript’s present no-cue combination produced \(P=-.455\), 95% Monte Carlo interval \([-.521,-.388]\). The maintained category cue alone produced positive protection, \(P=.617\ [.567,.666]\), and all three controls together produced \(P=.792\ [.738,.846]\). Some extreme no-cue settings improved weaker reminder-only or endpoint contrasts, but those improvements accompanied large general recall increases and did not constitute the selective-interference interaction.

**Reply 3 — What I have decided**

> The revision will say plainly that, within the evaluated fixed fitted regime, maintained film-category support is required for the modeled selective-interference interaction. Start-context reinstatement and monitoring are not being discarded: they can increase recall, can operate alongside the maintained cue, and produce different predictions about where retrieval begins and what happens after a nonfilm candidate is sampled. They are nevertheless not equally capable of explaining the relative protection of deliberate recall in this analysis. I am making this distinction because broadly raising film recall is not the same as selectively reducing the extra task cost produced by reminder-linked competitor learning. The necessity claim will remain explicitly limited to this grid and fitted base regime, not generalized to every possible eCMR parameterization.

**Reply 4 — What changes in the manuscript**

> In the new Simulation 3, immediately after the full reminder × task-strength × retrieval-condition result, I will define the three-way selective-protection contrast and report the grid range, simulation count, 0/121 result, current no-cue result, category-cue result, and all-controls result. Figure 8 and its caption will be rewritten so that its low-to-high endpoint comparison is not called the selective-interference interaction. No additional sensitivity heat map will enter the next main-text draft; the complete surfaces and secondary contrasts remain available in the reproducibility package. The current separate diagnostic figures will be consolidated: one panel will compare input-position curves across the control settings, and another will compare return to film after a nonfilm output. The Discussion will distinguish explaining selective protection from identifying potentially co-occurring operations.

## 6. What the account says about imagery-competing tasks

**Anchor:** Reply to Rik’s page-59 comment asking why Tetris should be more effective than a verbal task and whether Tetris images themselves intrude.

**Reply 1 — How this came up**

> Emily asks that the modeled film items be connected to brief hotspot moments or mental images rather than treated as undifferentiated whole films. Rik asks what would make Tetris more effective than an equally engaging verbal task and whether participants experience Tetris-image intrusions. The August meeting connects these questions: remembering a film fragment and actively imagining where a Tetris block will rotate or land are content-distinct events, but both may be represented at the broader level of visual imagery. The issue is which parts of that explanation are implemented by the present model and which remain theoretical proposals.

**Reply 2 — What I have decided**

> The account will distinguish three contributions. Engagement can make task representations strongly learned. Overlap in visual format can make actively generated task imagery relevant as a competitor to film-hotspot imagery. Distinctness in source and content can nevertheless allow a maintained film-directed cue to favor film images during deliberate recall. The current simulations implement the first contribution through task-association strength and the third through film-category support. They do not explicitly represent or manipulate visual-format overlap. I am choosing this formulation because engagement alone cannot answer Rik’s verbal-task comparison, while treating film and Tetris representations as identical would misstate Emily’s point. They may overlap in format while remaining readily distinguishable in content and source.

**Reply 3 — What changes in the manuscript**

> At the first model-to-paradigm mapping, film items will be defined as brief hotspot moments or mental images, and task items as abstractions of actively generated task imagery such as anticipated block rotation and placement. Results language will continue to say what follows when task learning is strong and film-directed support is discriminating; it will not claim that the simulations demonstrate why visuospatial tasks are uniquely effective. The Discussion will state: “Imagery-competing tasks may generate strongly encoded task imagery that overlaps with film-hotspot representations at the level of visual format, making it a relevant competitor, while remaining sufficiently distinct in source and content for a maintained film-focused cue to preserve deliberate recall.” It will then propose matched imagery-versus-verbal tasks and direct measurement of task-image intrusions. Emily’s unidentified paper remains a genuine citation task; no source will be invented to fill it.

## 7. The positive neutral-material prediction

**Anchor:** Reply to Rik’s existing comment on “Emotional memory gives source organization…” on page 13.

**Reply 1 — How this came up**

> Rik asks what the emotional CMR adaptation contributes and suggests simulating neutral material, potentially instead of retaining recognition. The August meeting distinguishes an uninformative result—emotional items being better remembered or clustering together—from the relevant question of whether emotionality changes the selective-interference interaction. My subsequent clarification was that the paper’s positive proposal is not that emotion creates the dissociation: the same effect should be possible with neutral targets and competitors when reminder-linked learning makes them contextually connected but a retrieval cue can still distinguish them.

**Reply 2 — What I have decided**

> I will not add an emotional-versus-neutral simulation to either the main Results or the supplementary work for this revision, and it will not replace recognition. Instead, the paper will make a positive generality prediction: emotionality is not required for selective interference. Neutral intervention material should produce the same qualitative dissociation when it becomes associated with target context strongly enough to compete during involuntary retrieval, while remaining distinguishable by information available to a deliberate target-directed cue. Emotion may change baseline accessibility, clustering, or effect size, but the account does not treat emotion alone as sufficient or emotion as necessary. I am choosing this because merely turning off an emotional parameter would be a weak robustness demonstration; varying target–competitor discriminability is the stronger test of the proposed mechanism.

**Reply 3 — What changes in the manuscript**

> The Abstract will add one sentence stating that the account predicts selective interference with neutral material when competitors are contextually linked but remain distinguishable by the retrieval goal. The Introduction’s emotional-CMR paragraph will explain that affective coding instantiates source organization in the present simulations rather than supplying the necessary cause of the dissociation. The Method and Results will state that emotionality is held fixed and is not tested. The Discussion will give the full neutral-material prediction and acknowledge that it remains untested here. The proposed neutral category-recall experiment later in the Discussion will be identified as the substantive future test, because it manipulates competitor binding and cue discriminability rather than merely asking whether neutral items are remembered less well.

## 8. Total-event versus unique-item scoring

**Anchor:** Add a new Jordan root comment to the page-45 sentence “The model therefore simulates a selective interference effect…”, immediately after the current Simulation 2 anchor figure. After reordering, this analysis will belong to Simulation 3.

**Comment 1 — How this came up**

> The August meeting notes ask whether the selective-interference effect changes when repeated intrusions are counted separately from unique intrusions. Later examination of McConnell clarified the underlying measurement concern: involuntary-memory measures often count every recurrence, whereas deliberate-memory scores often count an item or detail only once. That mismatch could exaggerate a dissociation even if the same contents remain accessible. Rik’s nearby Word comment invoking McConnell concerns maintained cueing in the vigilance task, so I should not attribute this scoring question to that comment. This is a meeting-derived robustness question that needs its own provenance thread.

**Comment 2 — What I have decided**

> I will report the total-versus-unique analysis as a compact robustness check in the main manuscript, not as a separate numbered simulation and not only in a supplement. With repeated retrieval enabled, total-event scoring produced a selective-protection contrast of 1.061, whereas unique-item scoring produced .780: unique scoring retained 73.5% of the effect. The repeated-event component was .281. I therefore conclude that recurrence increases the size of the modeled dissociation but does not manufacture it. I will not claim that the intervention specifically reduces repeated real-world intrusions, because the model permits recurrence within one uninterrupted retrieval sequence rather than modeling episodes separated across hours or days.

**Comment 3 — What changes in the manuscript**

> The Method for the new Simulation 3 will add a short scoring-robustness paragraph: repeated retrieval was enabled, the same factorial design was rerun with 5,000 trials per cell and ten paired batches, and film output was scored as total events, unique identities, and repeated events. Immediately after the behavioral anchor result, the Results will report total \(1.061\ [.967,1.155]\), unique \(.780\ [.704,.856]\), and repeated \(.281\ [.235,.328]\), identifying the intervals as Monte Carlo uncertainty. No new figure or table will be added. The Discussion paragraph beginning “Recall-based comparisons…” will explain why matched scoring matters and state the qualitative robustness conclusion. The 54.7% trial-recurrence and 14.3% immediate-repeat diagnostics will remain audit information rather than manuscript claims.

## 9. Replacing the old Discussion experiment

**Anchor:** Add a new Jordan root comment on “A dual-list externalized free-recall design would isolate these predictions…” on page 60.

**Comment 1 — How this came up**

> This paragraph contains an older two-list proposal. My 10 August design note contains a later proposal after the divider: a category-directed externalized-recall experiment using focal and alternative target categories plus a separate competitor list. No collaborator directly requested that exact experiment. It is a synthesis motivated by the broader general-memory framing, Rik’s questions about cue discriminability and mechanism-specific sequence signatures, and the need to observe target and off-target candidates under matched reporting conditions. The issue is therefore which of my own proposed designs should be carried forward, not which design the reviewers explicitly endorsed.

**Comment 2 — What I have decided**

> I will replace the older dual-list proposal with the later category-directed design. Participants first study blocked focal and alternative categories—for example, birds and occupations. Later material is always a potential competitor and is never itself a retrieval target. A postcue asks participants to recall either the focal category or the alternative category while externalizing every candidate that comes to mind. The same focal bird items are therefore correct targets when birds are requested and off-target list intrusions when occupations are requested. I am choosing this design because it keeps competitor material separate from the retrieval goal, holds the reporting procedure constant, and makes it possible to test whether a target-directed cue protects focal recall from reminder-linked competitors.

**Comment 3 — Exact design being carried forward**

> The core design crosses three factors. First, a focal episodic reminder versus a matched nonfocal control manipulates whether later competitors are encoded in focal-associated context; the reminder must not simply present the bird category label and semantically prime the targets. Second, competitor material is relatively cue-distinct or cue-overlapping—for example, furniture versus mammals, with several normed and counterbalanced category sets required in an actual experiment. Third, the retrieval postcue targets the focal category or the alternative category. Participants report and source-classify all candidates, with identity, input position, output position, and latency recorded. A matched reminder-only/no-competitor condition is required to identify any direct effect of reminding, and competitor memory is tested only after the primary recall test.

**Comment 4 — Prediction, scope, and manuscript treatment**

> With cue-distinct competitors, focal-context reminding should reduce focal items more when they appear as off-target outputs during alternative-category recall than when they are deliberately targeted, producing the general-memory analogue of selective interference. With cue-overlapping competitors, the focal category cue should also support competitors, so deliberate focal recall should lose some of that protection. Competitor entries and output transitions can then test predictions about retrieval initiation, sustained category support, and monitoring. These off-target outputs are deliberately elicited list intrusions, not spontaneous involuntary memories in daily life; the Discussion must state that boundary. The current two-list proposal will be replaced by three concise paragraphs covering design, predicted interaction, and sequence diagnostics. The v10 figure will not be inserted in this revision because its diagram obscures the factorial structure. Stimulus norming, power, and preregistration belong to future study development rather than the manuscript’s structural revision.
